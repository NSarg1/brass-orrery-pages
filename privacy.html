Brass Orrery

Privacy policy · effective 17 October 2026 · for the Android app and the browser game

Brass Orrery is a game made by one independent developer, Narek (NSarg1). It has no accounts of its own, no ads and no in-app purchases. This page says exactly what it collects and why.

Your progress stays on your device

Your progress (brass, fittings, stars, worlds relit, settings and your best watches) is saved on your device, in the game's own storage. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its data, deletes it there. The browser game never sends it anywhere.

Google Play Games (Android app)

In the Android app, if you are signed in to Google Play Games, the game keeps a copy of that same progress as a saved game in your Play Games account, so a reinstall or a new phone brings it back, and the lights you light in the sky show there as achievements. The copy and the achievements are stored by Google, under your Google account and Google's privacy policy. The game itself never receives your name, email or any account details; it only reads and writes its own saved game. You can sign out of, or delete your data in, Google Play Games at any time from the Play Games settings on your phone.

Anonymous play statistics

To balance the game, it sends anonymous play statistics to PostHog, an analytics service, on servers in the European Union. They are game data only, for example:

  • a session began or ended: the kind of device (touch or mouse), the screen size rounded to 100 pixels, the language, whether it is the web game or the Android app, how long the game was open and where you left it, and how many sessions there have been with the days since the first and the last (counted on your device, which keeps only the day numbers it needs; no date is sent);
  • a watch began or ended: the star, how long it lasted, what ended it, alterations and powers used, brass gathered, how smoothly the game ran (frames per second);
  • during a watch: which alteration you took from the ones offered, and how long you took to choose;
  • the prologue watched or skipped, a tutorial step reached, the Workshop, the journey's map or the achievements opened, a world's result, the daily watch's result, the first time you reach a step (your first comet broken, your first dawn), what you chose after a watch, whether you read a watch's briefing or skipped it, a result card shared (only how: never the picture, the words or who it went to).

They contain no name, no email, no account, no advertising ID and no device ID, and the game stores nothing on your device to recognise you. You can turn them off at any time from the game's title screen. In a browser that sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, they are off.

PostHog receives your device's IP address when the statistics are sent, as any internet service does, and may use it to estimate a rough location (country and city) recorded with them. The game itself never asks for or uses your location.

Crash reports

The game does not send crash reports. If you choose Report a problem, the report is copied to your clipboard for you to send, or not, as you wish.

Challenge a friend

When you press Challenge a friend, the game makes a picture of your result and opens your device's share menu. You choose where it goes; the game never learns who receives it.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13, and it does not knowingly collect information from them.
